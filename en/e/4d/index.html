#Four-dimensional space (4D)

This page collects and organizes information about the fourth dimension: how to understand it, how to picture it, and other interesting things.

Join the discussion in the Telegram chat: https://t.me/metageometria (in Russian)

#What is it?

4D, four-dimensional space, the fourth dimension - an abstract concept of a space that generalizes the properties of 3D, 2D, 1D and 0D to dimension 4.

We think of four-dimensional space first and foremost as something as similar as possible to our three-dimensional space, but with 4 dimensions. It doesn't matter how it might be implemented in math or in programming; what matters is that it stays similar in spirit to the three-dimensional world.

You can find out how something should work in 4D by drawing an analogy for the step from 3D to 4D, based on your understanding of the step from 2D to 3D.

Also, you won't find any hypotheses here about the fourth dimension of our world holding heaven and hell or parallel worlds: that's completely uninteresting and unprovable, so go take that nonsense to Ancient Aliens.

As a mathematical model of four-dimensional space for calculations and simulations, Euclidean space and analytic geometry work perfectly.

#FAQ

#2What about time? Time is the fourth axis, right? That's what Einstein said.

Don't mix up apples and oranges. Four-dimensional space is first and foremost an abstract concept that exists on paper, in programs and in people's minds. 4D doesn't depend on our world in any way.

And time as the fourth axis is one of the practical applications of abstract four-dimensional space in physics. But what's used there isn't pure 4D, it's Minkowski space.

#24D is only time!

Saying that 4D is exclusively spacetime is as meaningless as saying that the number 2 means only those 2 stones, and not 2 joules, not 2 photons, not 2 bits, not 2 x's.

The number 2 is a much broader concept than its particular realization as two particular objects.

Likewise, 4D is a much broader concept than its realization as spacetime.

#2But I went to a 7D cinema!

That's marketing nonsense, and it has nothing to do with a real 7D space.

Of course, the extra sensations do add an extra dimension of sensation while you watch, but that, again, is just a special case of using multidimensional spaces in real life, and the fact that you've watched a "7D movie" doesn't mean you can imagine 7 spatial dimensions.

You might as well say that our eyes actually see not a 2D picture but a 3.7D one, since we see with two eyes, with depth, with color, with time.

#2Why 4D in particular, why not 5D or a gazillion-D?

Any space > 3D is already more interesting than 3D. And 4D is easier to visualize, compute and understand than 5D and all the higher spaces. So 4D is the sweet spot between interesting and simple.

#24D doesn't exist!

Claiming that 4D doesn't exist is as meaningless as claiming that the number 2 doesn't exist. 4D is an abstract concept that doesn't depend on our world.

If you want to say that "our world isn't a geometric 4D space", then say exactly that.

If you want to say that "our world doesn't work according to the idea of spacetime", then say exactly that too.

#2In 4D, will we see both the surface and the insides of all objects at once?!

Next you'll tell me we'll see things "from all sides at once". No: in 4D, just like in 3D and 2D, we'll see only the surface of 4D objects. But we will be able to see the insides of 3D objects, because there 3D will be flat, and vision will be 3D. It will work the same way we see the insides of 2D objects in 3D.

#2So what is 3D vision like?

Vision takes an image as input, and the dimension of the image determines the dimension of vision.

Now let's figure out: what are 0D, 1D and 2D images?

  • 0D is 1 pixel.
  • 1D is a one-dimensional array of pixels.
  • 2D is a two-dimensional array of pixels.

Then a 3D image is a three-dimensional array of pixels, or rather voxels. And it can be visualized like this:

We see this picture with our 2D eyes, so some details are hidden behind others. If we had 3D vision, we would see it just as clearly, with all its insides, as if we were looking at a flat picture.

Also, seeing with 3D vision is roughly like looking at a person and instantly seeing their CT scan. Yes, 4D beings see all our insides, just as we see all the insides of 2D beings.

#2We have two eyes, so what if we add a third one? And how many eyes would you need in 4D space?

Your screen is flat. No matter which eye you look at it with, it looks the same. And somehow, looking at it, you manage to see three-dimensional pictures, be it games, movies, etc. So we conclude that a single eye should be enough to see space.

But if you add more eyes, you simply gain the ability to see "distances" to objects. Extra eyes won't give you true 3D vision.

#2Well, when I eat some shrooms, I see sooo many spatial dimensions too!

No, these substances can't make the brain compute 4D geometric transformations so that it can then project them and draw them correctly. Substances can only give you gibberish, or a statistically plausible picture (the kind neural networks sometimes draw).

Now, if you had already understood 4D visually before that, had formed enough neural connections to recognize and picture it, and then took the substance - that's a different story.

#2Why can't we imagine 4D?

Because we have no visual examples. We can reconstruct 3D objects from their shadows because we've seen lots of 3D objects and their shadows (hello, neural networks). But we've never seen 4D objects, so we can't imagine what a 4D object looks like from its "shadow" (projection) alone.

#2Visually

People quite often ask something like this:

OK, fine, I get that a tesseract has M vertices, N edges, K 2D faces and L 3D faces, and I can even calculate how many a five-dimensional cube will have. But how do I picture it in my head? How do I rotate it in my mind and see the hypervolume?

At the moment, I personally don't know of anyone who has managed to imagine four-dimensional space the same way we imagine three-dimensional space.

To truly understand four-dimensional space visually, you need to learn to do the same visual operations we can do with three-dimensional space, and to understand what our familiar things would look like in an unfamiliar four-dimensional world.

I have a hypothesis that to understand 4D you need to play a 4D game projected onto a three-dimensional image, for example a 4D Minecraft. In this game, the result of rendering should be a 3D voxel image with perspective projection. Then we'll draw this 3D image on a 2D screen and try to make it out.

#Interesting stuff

#24D geometric shapes

You know about regular polyhedra and semiregular polyhedra in three-dimensional space, right? Four-dimensional space has plenty of its own too, and there's a whole website that visually shows what they look like from different angles, along with their mathematical properties and explanations.

The Regular Polychora

Honestly, I have no idea how they managed to calculate all these shapes, it's just mind-boggling. There must be some very complicated math behind it. Too bad they wrote the coordinates of the shapes in such an inconvenient format.

#24D Toys
4D Toys: a box of four-dimensional toys, and how objects bounce and roll in 4D Channel: Miegakure
5:29

This is a game where you can interact with 4D objects, including in VR.

The only thing I hate about this game is the projection they use. They use cross-sections - slicing an object with a hyperplane. The video shows this in detail.

I think that for simple visualization it does work, of course, but for understanding 4D space it doesn't work at all. For understanding 4D, perspective projection is perfect. It's exactly the one that fits, because too much of geometry and of spatial concepts in general is tied to how this projection works.

What's more, with their projection:

  • You can't look at the surface of a four-dimensional object, you can only see a small part of it;
  • You can't look into the distance

With this projection, you won't understand where your item fell, because you won't see what's to the side of you.
