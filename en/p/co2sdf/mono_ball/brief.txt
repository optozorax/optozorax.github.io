# Research task: an orientable "wormhole into itself" for a 3D world in R^5, as an implicit codimension-2 field

Work only inside this directory (`tmp/codex_rp3/`). Python 3 with numpy is available; write scripts here. Put your final answer in a Markdown report (it will be saved as `last_message.md`).

## Setting
We render curved worlds as embedded submanifolds of Euclidean space given *implicitly*: a 3D world W in R^5 is the common zero set of two smooth functions F = (f, g): R^5 -> R^2, and the zero set must be *regular* (grad f, grad g linearly independent at every point of W). A ray tracer walks along W by stepping in a tangent direction and projecting back with a Newton step using the 2x2 Gram system of the two (numerical) gradients. Coordinates: x = (x1, x2, x3) for the world directions, n = (n1, n2) for the two extra directions. Outside a compact region the world must be exactly the flat space {n = 0} (for instance F = n there).

Allowed relaxations, used already elsewhere: (a) the field may be defined by several closed-form "charts" in different regions of R^5, switched by a hard test, as long as every chart's zero set agrees with the world where it is used (gradients are then taken with the chart frozen at the evaluation point); (b) a chart may flip the sign of one component across a cut (a world that is one-sided needs this). A 1D root solve (a few Newton steps on a scalar equation) inside a chart is acceptable; a multi-dimensional nearest-point search is the last resort.

## What already works (2D worlds in R^4, for orientation)
A plane with a "mouth" (disc of radius R) whose throat is a flat tube glued to itself at its far end, every point to the opposite one (a Moebius strip): with u = x1 + i x2 and v = n1 + i n2, the throat is the flat Moebius band { |u|^2 + 4|v|^2 = R^2, arg v = 2 arg u + spin(|u|) }. Field: away from the centre, turn the normal plane by angle = 2 arg u + spin: s = Re(v e^{-i angle}), c = Im(v e^{-i angle}); there the world is "a plane with a squashed dome": world(smooth_union(s, ball(x1, x2, 2 s; R)), c) where world(solid, carrier) = (solid, carrier). Near the centre (|u| < R/2) the turned frame is undefined; there the chart is (ball(x1, x2, 2|v|; R), Im(u * conj(e))) with e = exp(i (arg v - spin)/2) (a sign cut where arg v jumps). This 2D version necessarily mirrors you (RP^2 is one-sided).

## The question
For 3D worlds, gluing the far end of a spherical throat S^2 x I to itself by the antipodal map gives R^3 # RP^3, which is orientable: a traveller comes back out of the same ball-shaped mouth, at the opposite point, NOT mirrored. (A donut-shaped mouth can also do this: throat T^2 x I with the end glued by a fixed-point-free glide reflection of the flat torus; the end is the twisted I-bundle over the Klein bottle.)

**Find an explicit construction of such an orientable 3D wormhole-into-itself in R^5 as an implicit field F: R^5 -> R^2 meeting the requirements above.** Preferably the ball-mouth version (R^3 # RP^3). Ideally: exactly flat R^3 outside a ball, a mouth, and a throat region of controllable length; formulas short enough to print on a web page; built from simple pieces (balls, smooth unions, rotations of the normal plane by angles depending on position, complex products such as u^2, etc.).

Known obstacles (so you do not rediscover them):
- The 2D trick (normal plane turned by twice the angle around the mouth, then the dome's top point "blown up" into a circle) relies on pi_1(SO(2)) = Z; around a sphere there is no such winding.
- The direct real blow-up of R^3 at a point, as a graph n = B(x/|x|), needs the core RP^2 embedded in the normal space alone, i.e. R^4, so codimension 4 (R^7). In R^5 the core RP^2 must use world directions too.
- One partial idea: the I-bundle X(t, d) = (t d + a d3 J(d1, d2, 0), b (d1 + i d2)^2) for d in S^2, t in R (J = rotation by -90 deg in the x1x2 plane) is an embedded R^3 # RP^3 (asymptotically flat, not flat). Its chart f1 = d3^2 + rho^2 - 1, f2 = x3 - t d3, written through u = x1 + i x2 and eta = (n1 + i n2)/b, is closed-form but singular on the "pole fibres" (eta = 0); there the world is a graph over x that needs a cubic solve. It is also not flat outside. Something cleaner would be very welcome; if you cannot beat it, make it work properly (flat outside, regular, all charts), and say so.
